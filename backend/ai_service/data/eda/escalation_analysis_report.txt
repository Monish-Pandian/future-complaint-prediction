8. RESEARCH RECOMMENDATION
------------------------------------------------------------

1. IS A SECOND HIGH-RISK TARGET USEFUL?
   YES. The binary target (future_complaint) has ~90% positive rate,
   making it unsuitable for limited-budget verification where officers
   need to prioritize truly anomalous events. A high-risk target with
   a lower positive rate (5-20%) would enable meaningful ranking.

2. STATISTICALLY REASONABLE CANDIDATES
   - Candidate A (count >= 2): 79.9% positive rate - TOO HIGH
   - Candidate B (count >= 3): 71.2% positive rate - TOO HIGH
   - Candidate C (count >= 5): 57.4% positive rate - TOO HIGH
   - Candidate D (count > rolling_4w): 41.8% positive rate - MODERATE
   - Candidate E (count >= 1.5x rolling_4w): 23.5% positive rate - GOOD BALANCE
   - Candidate F (count >= 2x rolling_4w): 15.3% positive rate - VERY SELECTIVE
   (See escalation_candidate_comparison.csv for exact rates)

3. SINGLE THRESHOLD VS COMPLAINT-TYPE-SPECIFIC
   Complaint types show VASTLY different distributions (see Step 2).
   A single absolute threshold (A, B, C) will be biased toward
   high-volume complaint types. Relative thresholds (D, E, F)
   adapt to each type's baseline and are preferable.

4. PERCENTILE-BASED THRESHOLDS
   Percentile-based (e.g., top 10% per complaint type per week)
   would ensure consistent positive rates across types and time.
   This is recommended for operational deployment.

5. BEST TARGET FOR LIMITED OFFICER VERIFICATION
   RECOMMENDED: Candidate E (future_count >= 1.5 x rolling_mean_4w)
   - Adapts to each complaint type's natural baseline
   - Positive rate ~16-29% across types, ~23% global (suitable for limited budget)
   - Temporally stable: 22.8%-24.6% across years 2020-2025
   - Captures meaningful escalation, not just high volume
   - Can be combined with percentile-based calibration per type

   ALTERNATIVE: Percentile-based target (top 10-15% per type/week)
   - Guarantees fixed budget allocation
   - Requires defining the ranking unit (community_area + sr_type + week)
